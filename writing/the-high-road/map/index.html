Henry & Polly

  1. 1786Henry born, Fairfax County, Virginia. Polly Patton born the same year, Virginia.
  2. ~1800sTo Clark County, Kentucky — probably the Wilderness Road through Cumberland Gap.
  3. 1807James Patton born, Kentucky (oldest son).
  4. 1820John Patton born 12 July, Clark County. Seven children in all, all born in Kentucky; youngest about 8 at the move.
  5. ~1832To Missouri, Henry about 46. River to Rocheport (likeliest), then north on the high ground of the Grand Divide. The axle broke east of Excello, and they stayed (family story).

The Macon County decades

  1. 1837–38Two 40-acre federal land patents, Section 35, Middle Fork Township (BLM records).
  2. 1850Farm: 40 acres improved, 40 timber; 3 horses, 2 oxen, 18 hogs, 400 bu corn.
  3. 1853–56Henry dies; James Patton 1854; Polly 1856. Henry and Polly buried in the Halley Cemetery, now lost.
  4. 1868Louisa Ann Thompson Halley (b. Louisa County, Virginia) buried behind Friendship Baptist.
  5. 1885William Lloyd Halley born.
  6. 1909–12Mildred, Earl, Arnold born to William Lloyd and Zelpha Cox.
  7. 1915Zelpha dies, 31.
  8. 1916William Lloyd marries Hattie Sexton, about 16.
  9. 1923Raymond born, 9 May.
  10. 1940William Lloyd still farming Section 35.
  11. 1969William Lloyd dies; farm leaves the Halley name after 130+ years.
  12. 1979Hattie dies, Macon; Oakwood Cemetery.
  13. 1995Christmas: Earl tells Alan the wagon story.

Hattie Sexton

  1. 1899Born 11 Nov, Dryden, Lee County, Virginia — the county that holds Cumberland Gap.
  2. 1904Sister Carrie born, Virginia.
  3. ~1907Move to Missouri, "about 8" (Aunt Barbara). Family story: she walked behind the wagon. Likelier: the railroad. Father Joseph Franklin Sexton, coal miner.
  4. 1910Census, 4 May: the Sextons in Narrows Township, Macon County, the Halleys' township.
  5. 1913Sister Hester born, Randolph County, Missouri.

Open questions: where the Sextons lived between 1910 and Hester's birth in Randolph County in 1913; Henry's river landing (Rocheport likeliest; Glasgow didn't exist until 1836). Probably resolved: Friendship Baptist organized in 1867, but beside an older farm cemetery, which explains James Patton's 1854 burial there (unverified).
