I asked an AI how my ancestors got from the Missouri River to Macon County, Missouri, in 1832. It gave me a route with stations, like a train schedule. The county histories didn't back it up.

So I took the people out of it. USGS elevation data, about 3,100 points between the river and Excello. Find the highest continuous path from Rocheport, and from Franklin, without being told where the roads are.

From Rocheport it climbs straight north onto the divide in four miles and stays there. Fifty-two miles. Neither path goes through Huntsville, where the AI sent them. The railroad took that ridge in 1858. Highway 63 took it later.

The map has both routes, the elevation, and Henry's land: alanhalley.com/writing/the-high-road/map/

The flyovers are a KML file for Google Earth Pro, the free desktop version. Double-click a tour and it flies the route about 800 feet above the ground, from Rocheport or from Franklin.
