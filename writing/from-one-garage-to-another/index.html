From One Garage to Another

Two hundred letters, two offers, the wrong choice, and a year in Pasadena.

Chicago · Pasadena · 1985 · October 2026
Back to writing

I had nearly starved for two years of graduate school after my vacation/internship in France.

I found a mailing list of 200 companies that might hire a newly minted nuclear engineer. I wrote a letter, used some markup language to create a mail merge with the 200 addresses and printed the 200 letters out on an IBM laser printer that was 20 feet long! Paper was fed in by the yard. And quickly consumed.

I expected to receive no replies. But I eventually received two interview offers, several months after receiving my degree.

I went to interview at Sargent & Lundy in Chicago and the Ralph M. Parsons Company in Pasadena in the same week. I received two offers. Same money. One in Chicago, one in Pasadena. I chose Chicago.

Chicago

I worked downtown, across from the Art Institute. I stayed with friends because I had no money. It was an hour or more on the bus to work after standing on the lake shore at the bus stop. They call the wind "The Hawk" - seemed apt to me.

I spent my days making lists of radiation shielding wall dimensions of a built nuclear power plant. As-built documentation. Not design work.

I'd buy a sandwich and eat it at the Art Institute at noon. I liked Calder's mobiles. I'd go back to work on my lists. Then suffer an hour or more bus ride home.

I asked my boss when I could do something other than as-built documentation, maybe design work?

"Nope, I'm going to make you a regulator."

"What's a regulator?"

"Someone that works with the NRC on licensing issues."

I gave notice. They sent me to a second-level manager, who offered me a raise to stay. I turned it down.

I called the guy in Pasadena and asked if his job was still open. He made me grovel for a bit but finally admitted that he had written the job description specifically to hire me and asked me when I could start. Next week, I told him.

West

So I went back to Missouri to plan my trip to California. I was broke again after paying back moving expense money to get to Chicago. Not exactly golden handcuffs.

An old Nissan was sitting behind my parents' barn. Brother Dan said it was his wife's first car and had been sitting for a couple of years. It had a broken head gasket and was held together only by rust. I put air in the tires, installed a new head gasket, and headed west. It's only 1,500 miles.

It was June. It didn't rain and the nearly bald tires didn't blow.

I picked up a hitchhiker in Arizona. He bought a six-pack of beer and we made it to California. He wanted out around San Bernardino to head north.

I took an exit ramp and the brakes were not slowing us down much. My passenger put his foot on the brake. He had no brake on his side. His foot went through the floorboard and he helped stop the car by shoe leather.

I then slowly cruised on to Pasadena alone.

Pasadena

I had little money when I arrived in Pasadena. I lived in a nice hotel on Colorado Blvd the first week. Then I moved to a cheaper hotel in a seedier part of Colorado Boulevard. The area was mostly porn shops and boarded up storefronts. I moved to a cheaper motel the next week, and the next, and the next. Then into the YMCA. I got a really cheap small room there. Every morning I'd put on a tie and sport coat and walk the few blocks to the new Parsons building in Old Pasadena. Parsons was the first new building in the redevelopment project.

I worked in a group of 12 nuclear engineers. We did mostly conceptual designs: an Idaho nuclear fuel reprocessing plant, a long term spent fuel storage facility. I was assigned to make a list of atomic weights for a new project. These weights had to be reviewed internally, then by another consulting firm and then by DOE. That was the approval process.

I worked with a guy in Pasadena learning MCNP for criticality calculations. He was near retirement. I had seen his name in comments in Morse CG. He started coding in the 1950s. We were determining how much floor slope was allowed in the many concrete cells of the multi-stage fuel reprocessing plant. Highly enriched fuel. A pipe break in a cell could spill fuel onto the floor. If the liquid was too deep, it could go critical. I enjoyed the work. I was still using an IBM 370, pretty much unchanged for the last ten years. I wish I could have learned more from him, but the Taiwanese managers were untrainable. Micro managers usually are, aren't they?

Saturdays I'd drive to Santa Monica and read on the beach for the afternoon. Because I was in California and had no money. The water was too cold for me. Those beach party movies I had watched in Missouri had lied to me.

After living in the YMCA a few weeks, I got paid. So I looked for a place to live in earnest. New one bedroom apartments seemed expensive. I found an ad in a newspaper for a "back house." I went to look at it. It was on the back of an estate. Big house in front, big pool behind it and the pool house behind the pool. The owner, Gina, called it a back house. It was a small guest house. I wrote a check for $1,500, two months rent, with $750 due when I moved in at the first of the month. The house wasn't ready at the first of the month, nor the next month.

I finally went to the police and told my story to a detective. I was the 16th person Gina had scammed. She had gone to court a few times. No one ever got their money back. The police had no case against her.

I called Gina every couple of weeks and received a new excuse. After two months, I called Gina one morning from my office. I told her I wanted my money. She said she would put a check in the mail. I told her she would deliver the check to me at work within one hour. She did. The check cleared.

Gina had my rent money, so I lived at the YMCA two more months. After Gina repaid me, I found the room in an old garage. It was the Dr. Nathan H. Williams Carriage House in Altadena, built in 1916. Architects were Greene and Greene.

The garage had been separated from the main house. The living room was where the Duesenberg had been parked. I had the former maid's quarters, a small bedroom and bath next to the now living room. The galley kitchen had been an "oil room". And the former chauffeur's quarters, a bedroom and bath upstairs, was for the owner of the house. And the pool had been split off from the main house and sat on the separate lot with the garage.

The owner was an international tour guide. He was seldom at home. When he was at home, he would regale me with stories of his travels. He took me to a client's party in Long Beach. Everyone there had a travel story to tell. I found them fascinating. The owner had a curved glass cabinet full of Waterford crystal that he had brought home from his travels a piece at a time over many years. The pool was surrounded by many different types of plants, from cuttings he had brought home and planted over the years. It was a jungle.

I think the rent was $600 a month, less than the $750 plus utilities for a new one bedroom apartment. My salary was $35,000 a year.

The manager left on a month-long sales trip to Asia, and his assistant took over the group and piled on more work than anyone could do. When the manager came back, I brought him a page listing every task I'd been given and told him I couldn't do quality work with someone asking when each one would be done while handing me more. He tore the page up, threw it in the trash, and told me to do only the atomic weights. The next day he came into my office with new tasks. I typed my resignation. A second-level manager offered me a raise to stay. I turned it down.

After I bought the CRX, I parked the old Datsun in the Parsons parking lot. It was gone in days. It probably didn't go far.

I left Pasadena in the CRX in late 1985. I didn't see the Rose Parade.

Palm Springs

After living in the Altadena garage, I drove to Palm Springs. Jimmy had a one bedroom, 4,000 square foot house with three guest suites and a black bottom pool on a half acre. I stayed in one of the guest suites for a while.

The three guest suites were attached to the garage. An odd layout, but I knew nothing about Palm Springs.

I had moved from one garage to another.
