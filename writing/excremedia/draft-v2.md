‹**Excremedia** (n.): social media, from the point of view of someone trying to tell people what he built.›

If I had gone to sleep in 1986 and awakened in 2026, this would seem like software hell. Unfortunately, it's not a bad dream.

‹In December 1984 I finished my master's in nuclear engineering. I got a list of about 200 companies that might hire a nuclear engineer, did a mail merge on an IBM 370, and mailed 200 letters. I expected nothing. I got two interviews and two offers.› I called it marketing.

Everything was going along nicely for a while. I used Macs from 1987 to 1996. Then Windows 95 came along. I used whatever Dell laptop with whatever bug-ridden version of Windows I was given at all the organizations I worked at as a freelance IT PM, 1996 to 2007.

I had too much gray hair to get a contract job in Silicon Valley in 2006. Eight face-to-face interviews and no offer. So I moved to a beach in Brazil and started an iPhone app dev agency in 2008, back on Macs. That lasted until 2015, when it seemed like all the mobile apps had been built. In 2009 I had a marketing idiot create Facebook, Instagram, Twitter and LinkedIn accounts that I hardly used. I did Upwork and Fiverr gigs until 2020. Toptal told me I was unqualified several years ago. I never did understand Agile or Scrum.

This year, I built 11 software projects (8 still live), published three physics papers on Zenodo, and wrote 26 essays. This has been my most productive year ever.

And this year I have tried all sorts of software that doesn't work: excremedia.

‹My AI writes the code, runs the physics and keeps my records. It isn't allowed to post for me: LinkedIn's API terms forbid using the API "to automate posting."› I have to be confused by all the flashing lights and noise. I quit playing pinball machines years ago. They were more fun.

‹Hacker News killed my last three submissions on arrival. No reason given; I found out by logging out and looking.› HN has a DOS-like interface. ‹On a dead post there's no comment box at all.›

‹Mastodon disabled my account. No email, no explanation.›

‹Reddit wants weeks of karma before a post survives the robot moderator.›

Fiverr and Upwork don't work now. ‹A new listing can't be seen until it has the scores that only come from being seen.›

YC Confounders doesn't have a search. All the Confounders want to marry an engineer, but can't even build an MVP, an afternoon's work in 2026.

‹In 1984, 200 letters got me two offers. In 2026 I can build the product in an afternoon.›

Now I'm AI-pilled, trudging through social media sewage. Wading through the sewage has been quite unpleasant.

Excremedia.
