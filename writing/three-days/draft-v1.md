# Three Days

When I ran product acquisitions, I gave vendors two to three weeks to answer a request for proposal. They needed it. A proposal was real work: someone had to read the requirements, think, and write. The effort showed. A vendor who sent forty careful pages wanted the business. A vendor who sent four pages of boilerplate told you something too.

This morning I found a job on Upwork six minutes after it was posted. By the time I'd copied it, at eleven minutes, it had more than fifty proposals.

Nobody wrote fifty thoughtful proposals in eleven minutes. AI wrote them. They're fluent, polite, and they all claim exactly the experience the job asks for. Writing a proposal used to cost the vendor something. Now it costs nothing, so it no longer tells you anything.

I've hired a few hundred people over thirty-odd years, a lot of them developers, some of them on Upwork for clients. If I posted a job today, I'd wait three days before reading any of it. The first wave is the bots. The people who actually read the post show up after that.

Then I'd stop reading cover letters. Every post would carry one screening question, specific to the job, that a generic answer can't fake. The question isn't a quiz. It's a filter: anyone who answers something else is out in seconds. One question saves you from reading two hundred letters.

And I'd look for what's still expensive to produce: proof. A live product I can click through. Code that runs. A deployed demo. When I apply for work now, I lead with a link to software that's live and taking real payments, because a link is the one thing a language model can't generate for a candidate who doesn't have one.

Last, I'd hire for a small paid first milestone, a week or so, before committing to the whole project. You learn more from one week of delivered work than from any interview.

None of this is new. Wait for the real applicants, ask one good question, believe what you can verify, and start small. It's what we did when proposals took three weeks. The difference is that the proposal itself used to be part of the proof. Now it's just text.
