France, 1982: Barcelona and Mallorca

Part 3 of 5.

France · 1982 · October 2026
Back to writing

The landlady said I should see the Normandy beaches where so many Americans died, so we planned a trip to Normandy, leaving after work on a Friday. She, Jean-Pierre and I started north, but she heard on the radio that the weather was going to be bad in Normandy, so we should go to Barcelona instead. We turned around and went back to the house for the stuff we needed for Barcelona, whatever that was.

Jean-Pierre and I drove the German Ford through the night, switching drivers every two hours. Getting coffee then beer with every driver change. We arrived at a small town near their condo close to Barcelona just after dawn.

The landlady told me to pull over at a bar. I hadn't slept, having driven every other two hours through the night, but I thought that if the old woman (that's how she referred to herself) could have a drink at dawn, then I could too. We stood at the bar. She ordered. A small glass of green liquid was placed in front of me. I looked at it skeptically. She told me to drink it; I'd feel better. I did. She was right.

We bought some groceries and went to the condo on a protected little bay. Jean-Pierre and I went for a swim in the refreshing cool water, then I slept the rest of the day.

The next day we went into Barcelona. We walked La Rambla. Two girls beckoned me to go down an alley. Jean-Pierre caught my arm and told me it was very dangerous to go down an alley with those girls. I reluctantly continued down La Rambla with Jean-Pierre.

Jean-Pierre asked me if I liked calamari. I didn't know what calamari was.

"You must try it."

So he bought some fried rings at a kiosk. They were absolutely delicious!

"What am I eating?"

"Calamari."

"No, the English word."

He didn't know.

Then the old woman decided she wanted to go to Mallorca, so we took a night ship to the island. We arrived early morning. She had met her husband (Jean-Pierre's father) here and wanted to be alone for a while. Jean-Pierre and I were left to fend for ourselves.

He had been here before, so we just walked for a while. I spotted a bar and said I'd like to have a beer. Jean-Pierre said that a cold beer in the hot sun was not a good idea. We had a beer and walked on up a hill to a fish market under a big tent. Tables piled high with fish. We walked through the market. The smell of the fish in the heat was getting stronger as we walked. It was mid-day. The smell, the heat, the beer. I was getting queasy.

Jean-Pierre stopped and pointed at a big pile of octopus.

"That's it!"

"That's what?"

"Calamari."
