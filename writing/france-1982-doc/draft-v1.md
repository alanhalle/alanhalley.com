# France, 1982: Doc

Eight in the morning in France was when the bars closed in Missouri, so I called one of my night-owl friends a few times. One time, Doc answered the phone. He asked about France. He said he might come for a visit.

He arrived at CDG a couple weeks later. I picked him and a girlfriend up, and the landlady gave them her bedroom for a few nights. I gave them a tour of Paris. I knew my way around after several weekends in Paris.

Then we decided to go to Greece, as it was rainy in Paris in October. Doc's girlfriend was with us. She didn't say much. She was almost invisible. We drank huge mugs of beer in Munich, saw the castle that inspired Disney and headed south, intending to stop in Austria for lunch.

The car died in Germany early one morning, before we crossed Austria. A guy offered to look at it. He got it running again. I don't know what he did. I don't speak German. He wouldn't take any money.

We crossed Austria before lunchtime, so quickly that we didn't stop to eat. It was still cloudy, so we continued driving south through Italy. It began to rain. We were in the mountains somewhere in Italy. A hillside collapsed and sent a wall of muddy water against the car, pushing the car almost off the road. The rain let up as we passed the sign for Rome. We kept driving and ended up in Minori somehow. Not quite to Greece.

Doc had told me upon arriving that he had left the cash for the trip in his girlfriend's Bible in Texas.

"No problem, Doc. I have some money; we'll go on that until it runs out."

My money was almost out in Minori.

"Okay, Doc, it's your turn."

"I'll have someone wire me the money."

We got a room in a fourth-class pensione in Minori. And found out that there was a bank clerks' strike on and all the banks were closed.

Where can we get money wired to us? Only at a bank, but the banks are closed. Have been for two weeks.

When will the banks open? No one knows.

After learning about the bank clerks' strike, we went to Naples to try to get an international wire transfer. The banks in Naples were closed, too. It was a nationwide strike.

Doc asked if I had any more money.

"Just some traveler's checks in France."

"Do you have the serial numbers?"

"Yes."

"You could report the checks stolen."

So we tried that at a bank. The bank needed a police report. We went to the police. I told a policeman that I had lost my checks.

"What do you mean, 'lost'? Were they stolen?"

"Yes, they were stolen."

We had parked the car near a bank and discovered that a back window had been broken and my new red North Face bag had been stolen. The other bags were still in the car. I said the checks were in the bag.

He sent me to the US Consulate. They offered no solution.

So we drove back to Minori and checked in to the pensione again, explaining that my clothes and money had been stolen out of the car in Naples.

"Didn't you pay the Mafia guy on the corner 1,000 lira (about one dollar)?"

"No."

The desk clerk laughed at us, took our passports and gave us a room key. We could pay when we checked out.

When we went out for dinner, everyone was asking what happened to us in Naples. We told the story. They laughed, told us we were stupid Americans, and bought us beers.

We completely ran out of money in Minori. Doc found a retired Englishman who loaned him $100. The Englishman kept the flawed one-carat diamond ring that Doc had given me because I "needed some flash." Doc had to go back to the Englishman for $50 more before the banks opened. That was thousands in lira.

Doc had to buy cigarettes every day. I didn't smoke. I complained about his habit. I told him it was cutting into our food and beer budget. He told me that cigarettes came before eating and drinking.

We stayed in Minori for a week. Nice place. Great seafood restaurant up on a hill with spectacular sea views. The Italians were friendly. Not a bad place to be stranded.

When the banks opened, Doc got his money. He repaid me for his share of our trip expenses. Doc decided to stay in Italy a bit longer. I needed to get back to work after my longer-than-planned vacation. I left Minori early the morning after Doc's money arrived. I drove to Interlaken and bought some clothes. I had been wearing the same clothes for over a week. I went to Jungfraujoch, then back to Saint-Mammès. At work, my boss told me that the extra days of my vacation were covered. I had been working 40-hour weeks, and France had a 38-hour work week. A paid vacation!
